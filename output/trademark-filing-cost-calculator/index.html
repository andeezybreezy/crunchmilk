Trademark Filing Cost Calculator

Calculate the cost to register a trademark

What Is the Trademark Filing Cost Calculator?

The Trademark Filing Cost Calculator estimates what registering a trademark with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) actually costs, from the government filing fee through optional attorney help. Enter the number of international classes your goods or services fall under, choose your filing basis — TEAS Plus at roughly $250 per class or TEAS Standard at roughly $350 per class — and indicate whether you plan to hire a trademark attorney. The calculator multiplies the per-class fee by your class count, adds an attorney fee if you selected one, and returns a filing fee, an attorney fee, and a total. A business filing a single wordmark in one class under TEAS Plus pays $250 to the USPTO; the same mark filed in three classes (say, software, consulting services, and downloadable apps) costs $750 in filing fees alone before any legal help is added.

Why This Calculation Matters

Most people budgeting for a trademark filing think only about the headline USPTO fee, then get surprised later. The per-class structure is the first surprise: filing fees aren't a flat rate — they multiply by every international class your goods or services touch, so a company selling both a physical product and a related software app may need two or three classes, not one, and the fee scales accordingly. The second surprise is that the filing fee is a floor, not a ceiling. TEAS Plus requires you to select goods and services descriptions straight from the USPTO's own ID Manual and to authorize email correspondence; if your description doesn't match exactly, the USPTO can require the TEAS Standard fee instead or issue an office action, and responding to an office action typically costs another $300–600 in attorney time even when the mark itself is fine. The third, and largest, hidden cost is what happens after registration: a Section 8 declaration of continued use is due between years 5 and 6 (with its own per-class USPTO fee), a combined Section 8/9 renewal is due every 10 years, and if anyone opposes your mark during its 30-day publication window, contested proceedings before the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board can run into the thousands. As a worked example: a small business filing in 2 classes via TEAS Plus at $250 per class pays $500 in USPTO fees; add a flat-fee attorney package around $600–$1,200 for a clearance search and filing, and total upfront cost lands roughly $1,100–$1,700 — before accounting for a possible office action response, which can add another $300–$600 if the examiner raises a classification or likelihood-of-confusion issue.

When Should You Use This Calculator?

Run the numbers before you file, not after — trademark applications are decided win or lose, and the USPTO keeps your filing fee even if the application is ultimately refused. Use the calculator while you're still deciding how many classes to file in, since each additional class is a real, multiplying cost rather than a rounding error, or while comparing a DIY TEAS Plus filing against a flat-fee attorney package. It's equally useful when budgeting a startup's first-year legal spend alongside incorporation and contracts, and mid-process too: if an office action arrives, re-run the total with an estimated response fee added so you know the real cost of seeing the application through rather than abandoning it.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter Your Number of Classes: Enter how many international classes your goods or services fall under. Each class is billed separately, so an accurate count here drives most of your total cost.
  2. Choose Your Filing Type: Select TEAS Plus or TEAS Standard. TEAS Plus has the lower per-class fee but requires a pre-approved goods and services description; TEAS Standard costs more per class but lets you write a custom description.
  3. Indicate Whether You're Using an Attorney: Choose Yes if you plan to hire a trademark attorney for a clearance search and filing; the calculator adds a typical flat-fee range on top of the USPTO cost.
  4. Click Calculate: Hit the Calculate button to see your USPTO filing fee, attorney fee, and total upfront cost, computed instantly in your browser.
  5. Review Your Results and Plan Ahead: Check the breakdown, then factor in possible downstream costs — an office action response or the Section 8 declaration due in years 5–6 — that aren't part of the initial filing total.

How It Works

This calculator adds the USPTO's per-class government fee to any attorney fee you specify: Total = (Per-Class Filing Fee × Number of Classes) + Attorney Fee. TEAS Plus filings run about $250 per class and TEAS Standard about $350 per class under recent USPTO fee schedules, and the gap matters because TEAS Plus requires an ID Manual description and electronic correspondence in exchange for the discount. Filing basis also affects the total: an intent-to-use application later needs a Statement of Use, which carries its own USPTO fee (roughly $100–150 per class) once you actually start using the mark in commerce.

The basic rule:

  • Total Cost — Total = (Per Class Fee × Classes) + Attorney Fee — Government filing fee plus optional legal representation.

USPTO fees are set by federal regulation and do change periodically — confirm the current per-class rate at uspto.gov/trademarks before you file, since this calculator reflects recent published fee schedules rather than a live feed. This tool estimates cost only; it isn't legal advice. A trademark attorney can evaluate your goods-and-services classification, run a proper clearance search for conflicting marks, and advise on your odds of registration in ways a calculator can't.

Tips & Considerations

  • Pull your goods and services description straight from the USPTO's Trademark ID Manual before choosing TEAS Plus — a non-matching description can bump you to TEAS Standard pricing or trigger an office action.
  • Don't guess at your class count. A single product line can span multiple classes — physical goods in one, a companion app in another — and each one multiplies your filing fee.
  • Get an attorney's clearance search quote before filing, not after — a conflict found pre-filing costs far less to fix than one found via an office action or opposition.
  • Set aside a contingency for a possible office action response, roughly $300–600 in attorney time, rather than treating the filing fee as your only cost.
  • Calendar the Section 8 declaration window (years 5–6) and the 10-year renewal now — missing either one cancels the registration regardless of how much you spent to get it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it actually cost to register a trademark with the USPTO?

USPTO filing fees run about $250 per class under TEAS Plus or $350 per class under TEAS Standard, per recent fee schedules — a single-class wordmark filed via TEAS Plus costs $250 in government fees alone. Add attorney help and total upfront cost for a straightforward one- or two-class filing typically lands between $600 and $1,700, depending on whether you file yourself or hire counsel for a search-and-file package.

Why does filing in more classes cost so much more?

Each international class covers a distinct category of goods or services, and the USPTO charges its per-class fee separately for every class you include — it isn't a flat application charge. A company registering a mark for both a physical product (say, Class 25 for clothing) and a related app (Class 9) pays two full per-class fees, not one shared fee, because the USPTO examines each class independently.

What's the difference between TEAS Plus and TEAS Standard?

TEAS Plus costs less per class but requires you to select your goods and services description directly from the USPTO's ID Manual and to agree to file and correspond electronically; deviate from an approved description and the USPTO can require the higher TEAS Standard fee instead. TEAS Standard costs more per class but lets you draft a custom description, which is sometimes necessary for less common goods or services.

Is the filing fee the only cost, or are there fees later?

The filing fee covers only the initial application. If your mark is based on intent to use, you'll later need a Statement of Use (roughly $100–150 per class) once you're actually selling under the mark. After registration, a Section 8 declaration of continued use is due between years 5 and 6, and a combined Section 8/9 renewal is due every 10 years — both carry their own USPTO fees, and missing either one cancels the registration.

Do I need an attorney to file a trademark application?

No — the USPTO allows individuals and businesses to file their own applications through TEAS, and many small businesses do it themselves for a single, low-risk mark. That said, an attorney adds real value beyond paperwork: a proper clearance search to catch conflicting marks before you file, correct classification to avoid a downgraded or rejected application, and representation if an office action or opposition arises — judgment calls a calculator can't make for you.

What happens if the USPTO issues an office action?

An office action is a formal letter raising an issue — often a classification problem, a likelihood-of-confusion conflict with an existing mark, or a description that needs narrowing. You (or your attorney) typically have three months to respond. An attorney-handled response commonly runs $300–600 in additional fees, and the USPTO doesn't refund your original filing fee whether or not the office action is resolved successfully, so it's worth budgeting for this possibility rather than treating the base filing fee as your total cost.
